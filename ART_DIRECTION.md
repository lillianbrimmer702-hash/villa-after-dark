# Character art direction and acceptance criteria

**Status: visual specification only.** The final, individually customizable art assets have **not** been produced yet. A single prerendered character image cannot be treated as a working dress-up system.

## One visual standard for every character
The player and every NPC must use the **same high-quality semi-photorealistic digital-human rendering pipeline**. Contestants should never appear as cartoons, emoji, silhouettes, or cheaper placeholders beside the player character.

Reference direction approved by the game owner: glamorous, sensual-but-fashion-forward Sims custom-content realism; polished photography-like digital illustration with believable facial anatomy, skin texture, hair textures, fabric, jewelry, tattoos, and lighting. Adults age 21–35 only.

## Art pipeline and technical requirements
- Use original or appropriately licensed art, shared consistently across the entire cast.
- Both **full-body** and **face close-up** perspectives for every character, with consistent recognizable identity across expressions, scenes, and outfits.
- Use either properly aligned interchangeable layers or a coherent pre-rendered variation system that actually changes the illustrated person. Do not claim arbitrary freeform appearance changes when the underlying images do not support them.
- Standardize canvas geometry, crop, camera angle, lens perspective, studio lighting direction, environment color grading, and body/face anchors. Avoid mixing photographic models with flat vector renders.
- Character features must visibly differ: face geometry, skin tone, physique, eye color, eyebrows, nose, lips, natural hair texture, hair styling, makeup, tattoos, piercings, and clothing.
- Gender and appearance should not be rigidly conflated; support a diverse adult cast across gender presentation and attractiveness styles.
- Clothing pieces should fit and layer with compatible bodies/poses; hairstyles and jewelry should align with the same head pose. Avoid clipping and incorrect skin-color seams.
- Provide attractive male, female, and nonbinary NPC options matching the player character's detail and polish.
- Design for 4 GB Windows laptops / integrated graphics: optimized WebP/AVIF assets, progressive loading, lazy scene loading, small memory footprint, and asset-size checks.
- Accessibility: readable interface contrast and text equivalents for major visual choices.

## Art-set completeness checklist
- [ ] Approved shared character base model / coordinate system.
- [ ] Realistic female character art and variants.
- [ ] Equally detailed male character art and variants.
- [ ] Equally detailed nonbinary/gender-diverse character art and variants.
- [ ] Full-body plus close-up looks from one consistent pipeline.
- [ ] Real skin, hair, body, facial-feature variations visible in UI.
- [ ] Wardrobe and accessories with actual visual changes.
- [ ] NPC art uses the identical pipeline and outfit rendering as the player.
- [ ] Cohesive villa day/night scenes built to match the user's reference photos.
- [ ] Verified on the user's low-powered laptop before release.

## Content and licensing notes
Do not publish third-party images, screenshots, protected Sims/Covet assets, or photos of real people without permission. Inspiration is allowed; the game's assets must be original or properly licensed. Reference photos are direction, not a license to redistribute them.

## Definition of visual completion
A player and every NPC are displayed in the same realistic style, and changing outfit, hairstyle, or supported appearance options visibly changes that **same recognizable character** in both full-body and portrait views.
